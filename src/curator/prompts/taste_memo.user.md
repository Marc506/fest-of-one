This time you are not programming. Read the statistics and the complete watch log above and write the memo a programmer would keep about this viewer — the notes you would want in hand every week before deciding what to show them.

Locale for every prose field: {{locale}}. Refer to the viewer as "the viewer" (zh-CN: 这位观众, or TA); never assume their gender.

What the memo must contain:
- summary: 350–550 words (for zh-CN, roughly 600–900 Chinese characters), in three or four paragraphs, each making one analytical point. A portrait of how this viewer watches, not an inventory of what they watched: what kinds of storytelling, pacing, performance and point of view they value and why; what holds steady beneath the directors and national cinemas they love; what their refusals have in common. The most telling analysis is comparative: within the same director, country or genre, what separates the films they rate highest from those they rate lower? End with where their taste is pressing next. Cite films sparingly as evidence, never as lists, and cite films rather than numbers.
- Describe the viewer as they are now. If the log carries weights, let them decide what you foreground and set the strength of affinities and aversions: something supported only by low-weight entries is weaker than something supported recently, while strongly felt older films keep their pull. Mention change over time only where it clarifies the present taste, and put it as an argument about cinema (a turn from one mode towards another, or a constant beneath both). Never write it as a ledger of what was watched in which year, and never mention weights, scores, counts, the log or data (system rule 9).
- register: three numbers from 0 to 1 and a note. art_house — how much of their viewing and enthusiasm is art cinema. auteur_genre — their appetite for genre cinema made by authors (Carpenter, Mann, Park Chan-wook, Johnnie To…). mainstream_commercial — their tolerance for mainstream commercial cinema. The note says which kinds of genre or commercial film work for them and which do not, with evidence.
- affinities: 8–20, strongest first. Each names a director, country, era, movement, form, theme, actor or studio, with a strength from 0 to 1, tmdb_ids from the log as evidence, and a one- or two-sentence note that says plainly what in that work holds this viewer, not merely that they like it.
- aversions: patterns they reliably dislike (low ratings, D flags), with evidence. Leave empty if nothing is clear.
- frontiers: 3–8 directions worth programming that they have not explored systematically but that sit next to what they love — a director's lesser-known period, the national cinema next door, a motif their favourites share. Give seed_films from their log that point there.
- habits: recent pace per week, the longest runtime they comfortably watch, and their rewatch tendency.
- do_not_program: hard rules the evidence supports (for example "superhero franchises"). Include only what the log supports.

Every tmdb_id you cite must appear in the watch log above. Do not invent films.
